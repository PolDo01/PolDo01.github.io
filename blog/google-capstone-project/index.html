Google Capstone Project

Cyclistic bike-share — how casual riders and annual members use the bikes differently, cleaned in Excel/Power Query and visualized in Power BI.

From the Google Data Analytics Certificate capstone: a full business case for Cyclistic, a bike-share company in Chicago. Originally published on Medium.

About the company

In 2016, Cyclistic launched a successful bike-share offering. Since then, the program has grown to a fleet of 5,824 bicycles that are geotracked and locked into a network of 692 stations across Chicago.

The bikes can be unlocked from one station and returned to any other station in the system anytime. Until now, Cyclistic's marketing strategy relied on building general awareness and appealing to broad consumer segments.

One approach that helped make these things possible was the flexibility of its pricing plans: single-ride passes, full-day passes, and annual memberships. Customers who purchase single-ride or full-day passes are referred to as casual riders. Customers who purchase annual memberships are Cyclistic members.

Scenario

You are a junior data analyst working in the marketing analyst team at Cyclistic. The director of marketing believes the company's future success depends on maximizing the number of annual memberships.

Therefore, your team wants to understand how casual riders and annual members use Cyclistic bikes differently. From these insights, your team will design a new marketing strategy to convert casual riders into annual members.

But first, Cyclistic executives must approve your recommendations, so they must be backed up with compelling data insights and professional data visualizations.

Business task

Three questions will guide the future marketing program:

  1. How do annual members and casual riders use Cyclistic bikes differently?
  2. Why would casual riders buy Cyclistic annual memberships?
  3. How can Cyclistic use digital media to influence casual riders to become members?

Tools

  • Excel and Power Query for data cleaning
  • Power BI for visualization

Data analysis

  • Casual customers have 1.5 times the usage of rideables than members.
  • Both customer types increased bike usage in July 2022, with a drop at the end of November 2022.
  • Casual customers use the bikes mostly on Saturday and Sunday, while members use them mostly Tuesday to Thursday.
  • Bike usage during the day rises around 6:00 am and peaks around 5:00 pm for both groups.
  • Casual customers are the only users of docked bikes.
  • The top 5 starting stations and routes for members cluster around the city — at schools, universities, and business districts. This shows members mostly use the service to go to work and school.
  • The top 5 starting stations and routes for casuals cluster around the city shoreline, Elmwood Park, and Dolton — parks and tourist spots. This shows casual users mostly use the service for leisure.

Conclusion

  • Members use the service mostly for work and school.
  • Casual members use the service to visit parks and tourist spots in Chicago.

Recommendation

  • Provide marketing ads for casual customers at docking stations near key tourist spots in Chicago, e.g. Elmwood Park, Dolton Park.
  • Marketing ads should incentivize casual customers to get the service for daily/weekly/monthly usage. For example, promos for using the service at key times such as 6am–5pm daily, Thursday–Sunday, and March–June.
  • Use QR codes for bike unlocking/locking and promo ads.
